Stacks

Control matrix for the chord-progression tool on Ableton Push 3, a Push Hack catalog hack. Open it from Push Hack Home (Shift + Help): it takes Push's screen, encoders, buttons and pads; Live keeps the transport. The same six banks as on Move, with full names on a 960×160 screen and 64 pads that write the selected chord.

Pads — three pages, picked by the scene buttons

The bottom two rows are always the chords and the top two always the patterns. The middle four write the selected chord, and change with the page. Holding a scene button shows the screen below; the table is the same map, with the pads' LED colours.

Listen mode — Shift + LIVE

The pads, buttons and encoders stay with Live: play your instrument as usual. The screen names the chord being played, on split-flap tiles that flip as you add a note (C, CMAJ, CMAJ7), with its degree and role in the key, its inversion or slash bass, and what a voicing leaves out. One tile per note drops in and latches; the keys go down, the root in amber. Below: C E B in A minor, a Cmaj7 without its 5th.

Worth knowing

Open it from Push Hack Home.

Shift + Help opens Home, push-manager's switcher; pick STACKS. Star it there, or make it the superstar to open it with Shift + Help, Help. Shift + LIVE switches to listen mode; LIVE gives everything back. With an older Push Hack without Home, Shift + Help cycles Live, editing and listening, as in 0.2.

Live keeps the transport.

Play, Record, Metronome, Tap Tempo, the tempo and volume encoders go to Live, with Live's own LEDs. Stacks follows Live's clock.

Closed is parked.

When you close Stacks it stops playing along with Live; opening gives Run back. A stamp that is armed still plays.

Hold a chord pad to hear it.

The chord loops with its rhythm while you hold the pad, and a playhead runs inside it. Tapping only selects.

Hold a scene button for the pad map.

The screen shows every pad row of that page, named, in the pads' own colours, until you let go.

Lit means it does something.

Amber is where you are or what is on. White is available. Dark has no meaning here. The arrows light only when they can move.

Inversion can read AUTO.

Under a dyn Voice Grouping, voice leading picks each chord's inversion. The first chord, and a chord after a rest, keep their own.

The lanes hold still.

Voicing edits never zoom the staff in; it zooms out only to keep notes on screen, and moves by whole octaves. The bar at the right edge shows the register.

Slots show their chords.

A used pattern slot shows its chord count, bars and first chords. CLEAR SLOT or COPY TO SLOT, then tap the slot.

Sync once, in Live.

Settings > MIDI > Stacks In > Sync on. Without it Stacks cannot follow Play and Stop; the screen says so while Run is on.

An older Push Hack is named, not hidden.

If push-manager lacks pass-through buttons or raw frames, Shift + Help shows what to update. Playing into Live still works.

Shift + Scale follows Push's scale.

Stacks' Key and Scale follow the scale set with Push's Scale button, marked FROM PUSH on MAIN. A scale Stacks lacks keeps Stacks' and follows the key. Needs Browser Bridge 0.3, active in Live.

Listen mode names what you play.

Your pads stay with Live; the screen names the chord, with its degree, inversion and what is left out. Played voicings count: no 5th, a slash bass, add11, 7#11, two notes as an interval.

The power button always works.

push-manager gives the screen and buttons back to Push the moment you press it, and at every start, so Live's own questions can be answered.

Two ports, found by Live.

Stacks Out carries the chords to the selected track. Stacks In carries Live's clock in.
